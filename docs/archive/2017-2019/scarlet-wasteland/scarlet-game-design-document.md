# Game Overview

Oneness is the truth of the Academy, the only home that Scarlet has ever known. A life of simple, quiet meditation. Everything is… gray. Until an accidental brush with a powerful Artifact transports Scarlet to a world filled with color. Searching for a way back home, Scarlet jumps, dashes, slams, swings, and slides across strange and vibrant lands. Along the way, she's forced to grapple with the stories she's so used to telling about the world and herself.

Scarlet is a 2D platforming search for truth and the self. As the first game of an amateur studio, it will be released under the PWYW model to gain the trust of the gaming public. It will find its audience by clearly positioning itself as a story-driven game with a unique exploration of oneness and the self.

## Philosophy

There is a growing interest in mindfulness in the zeitgeist. Though there seems to be a new meditation app popping up every other day, I'm not aware of any games that explore the theme. Luckily, recent indie successes like GRIS and Celeste prove that there is both a commercial market and critical audience for games that explore complex issues of the mind. Hellblade also deserves mention; though not a platformer and arguably not "indie", it thoughtfully explores the experience of psychosis. My main interest in developing Scarlet is as an exploration of the concepts of the self and oneness.

### The Stories We Tell Ourselves

When one subjects the nature of their experience to directed attention, like that taught by mindfulness meditation, one can make several surprising discoveries. Once you get over how absolutely bonkers and out of control your thoughts are, you might stop identifying with these thoughts as much as usual. In other words, you are not your thoughts. But memories of the past and hopes for the future only exist as thoughts in the mind. "You" then seems to be simply a story, a collection of thoughts, that plays itself out on the stage of your consciousness. As you've already established, though, you are not your thoughts. Who, then, are you?

Scarlet starts the game with one story, that of an Academic. Her encounters with others in the world inevitably imprint themselves on her experience. She experiences the lives a desert nomad, a fugitive, an explorer. These are all stories that compete for the title of "Scarlet."

### Oneness and the Unity of Experience

In any given moment, we only have consciousness and its contents. This is true not only for humans but any conscious being. The Academy has taken this idea and turned it inwards, focusing on the experience of consciousness to the exclusion of all else. However, one might instead turn this truth outward and realize a sense of unity with all conscious creatures. All the joy, pain, fear, or beauty that you value in life is made possible by the fact that there is something that it is like to be you, and that is a statement that applies to every conscious creature. Each of us, whether Academic or desert nomad, is fundamentally seeking the same things in life, to find meaning and avoid suffering. If you strip away the story of self as discussed above, you might find just as much meaning in the joy and success of another as you would for yourself as, in either case, the joy is playing out against the same fundamental underlying experience: that of a conscious being.

# Game World

## Overview

* Hard to pin the time of the world. Advanced tech + ruins + nomadic life. Maybe it's a circle of discovery -> science -> artifact -> isolation -> ignorance -> destruction -> discovery
* The Artifact has drained the energy from the world. The further from the Artifact you are, the more bleak things get. Desert nomads remark on this, lament the loss of culture.

## Key Locations

### The Academy

There is only Being. All being is one, and all beings are one. This is the Truth and law of the Academy. One might call it an institution of science, or of religion, or of law. The truth, however, is that it is *the* institution; there simply are no others. And within it, everyone and everything is the same. Its halls are populated by a uniform and androgynous people who have never seen the world outside its seamless steel walls. This is where we first meet Scarlet.

The main occupation and/or pastime of the Academics is quiet contemplation of the Truth. There is no other knowledge worth seeking. Those with the deepest understanding of the Truth guide and supervise life in the Academy. This is not a position of bestowed authority of course, as all beings are one, but simply a natural consequence of their Knowledge.

Unknown to all but the oldest and wisest Academics, a powerful Artifact lies at the center of the Academy. The Academics tend to it through rituals whose meanings have long since been lost. It is not known if these ministrations are responsible for the endless energy put forth by the Artifact - making life in the Academy possible - or if they are simply ceremonies of thanks to a mysterious universe.

### Desert

Outside the Academy's walls, yet not so far as to be called remote, the nomadic desert people forge a life for themselves from the land. Where the Academics sit in quiet repose, the Travelers work and live and relish the living. They are not ignored by the Academics so much as simply forgotten, and there has been no interaction between the two people in memory or record. The Travelers have constructed power stations surrounding the Academy, feeding off the excess energies produced by the Artifact. They don't get too close, though, as stories of strange illnesses befalling those who do keep the rest at bay. A small merchant town has grown near the Academy. Groups of Travelers periodically return to this town to recharge and trade.

### The Fallen Library

> Image not included (third-party): a snowy-mountain inspiration painting of a small robed figure and a glowing light trail against a blizzard. No source URL given in the source document.

Before the Academy, science and discovery flourished at the Library. While it started as a single, simple library, a small city eventually blossomed around this mountain-top center of learning. It was at the library where the Artifact was developed. Key to its development was the understanding of underlying universality of reality. Matter and energy, space and time, all simply different manifestations or interpretations of the same thing. Different ground states of a pervasive and all-encompassing field of being.

After the development of the Artifact, life became paradise. With infinite energy, all material needs were met. Further scientific progress became pointless. Over time, society folded in around the Artifact, and the Academy was born. The Library, still filled with ancient truths, was abandoned and soon forgotten.

Scarlet journeys to the Library to understand what's happened to her. By this point, the city hasn't felt a human heartbeat in hundreds of years. The elements have weathered the structures to complete disrepair. The skeletal frames of buildings dot the mountain side. The Library itself has fared somewhat better, but it is by no means an exemplar of structural stability. A monumental building, traversed in ancient times with the use of elevators and trains, it stands as Scarlet's biggest challenge.

### Other Locations

Each level in Scarlet unveils a new color in the spectrum and a new ability for Scarlet. The world grows ever more beautiful even as the environmental puzzles Scarlet overcomes become more and more challenging. Each level also represents one of the elements. The first, introduced above, is the desert (fire). Others include:

* Mines (earth)
* Forest (wind)
* Sunken temple (water)

## Plot

On her way to start the day's first meditation, a young woman stumbles upon a previously unnoticed doorway in the familiar halls. Entering, she finds herself in a room empty save for a beatiful, glowing sphere floating in its center. Entranced, she approaches it when voices behind her signal the approach of the leaders of the Academy. She backs into the Artifact, and a brilliant glow overcomes her. When she is once again in possession of her senses, the voices are louder and more concerned. Whatever happened, it's been noticed. Feeling panic for perhaps the first time in her life, the woman notices a patch of wall with a property she's also never seen before: color, a vivid red. Overwhelmed, she rushes towards it and away from the approaching Academics.

Seeming to walk right through the walls of the Academy, she emerges near a desert power station. A maintenance crew had been dispatched to this particular station after a sudden energy burst knocked it offline. Completely unprepared for the harsh sun and heat, Scarlet stumbles into the outpost before collapsing.

She is taken back to the main camp and cared for until she comes around. When she does, she finds herself in a vibrant and terrifying new world. Against the warnings of the Travelers, she makes her way back to the Academy walls in hopes of a way back in. Instead, she finds seamless, uncaring steel. She continues her search until the conditions again take her under.

Back at the camp, she decides she has no choice but to stay with the desert people. She helps her caretaker - Kuluun, an aging farmer - and other townspeople with their work, often volunteering to trek to the various outposts for needed supplies, always hoping to spot a way back into the Academy. In doing so, she finds herself able to dash short distances with astonishing speed, completing the trips in record time. In recognition of her help, the townspeople give the woman a scarf to protect her bald head from the sun (all Academics are bald from birth). This scarf has the same strange property as the mysterious patch of wall in the Academy. Inquiring, she learns about colors, though at the moment she only sees red. Everything else is gray. She takes the color of the scarf as her name: Scarlet.

It's not long before Scarlet becomes curious about the world beyond the desert, a concept that so recently would have been impossible for her to conjure. One day, as she is working in a nearby mine, a strange metal man approaches Scarlet. The man, Slate, explains that he is from the Academy, which has fallen into disarray. He demands that Scarlet come back with him to return the Artifact. Bewildered, Scarlet explains that she doesn't have it and never has. As the two argue and Scarlet grows increasingly frustrated, she starts to notice the walls of the mine twinkling in that strange, particular way as the color yellow enters her consciousness. In a moment of frustration, Scarlet stomps her foot with terrifying force. On ominous rumbling begins, and the way back caves in. The two put the argument aside to escape from the mine. When a particularly treacherous ledge gives way, Slate falls with it. Scarlet risks her own life, using her dashing ability to save him.

They emerge on the edges of a forest. Scarlet convinces the man, Slate, that their only choice is to continue on together and try to find a way back. They trudge through the woodlands for a while with no clear progress when Scarlet has the idea to climb a tall tree for a better view. Distrustful of the world outside the Academy, Slate refuses to remove his metal enclosure and so is too heavy for some of the lighter branches. Scarlet reaches a vantage point, however, and notices a structure similar to the Academy near a body of water. At this exhilarating height, the beauty around her fills her mind and she learns of the color green.

As the two reach the structure, they discover it indeed resembles the Academy, though much smaller and now mostly submerged in a lake. The two are inevitably curious due to the similarity. However, Slate is unable to swim in his suit. Scarlet promises him that she will return after exploring the temple. She makes her way through its sunken halls with the help of her abilities, looking for another Artifact chamber. Instead, she finds writings about the development of the Artifact by scholars in a place called the "Library," atop a uniquely-shaped mountain. As she returns to the lake, she sees the blueness of its waters.

Scarlet explains what she found to Slate, and that she recognizes the mountain's description from the view she got above the trees. Perhaps there they might solve the mystery of the Artifact's disappearance? The two head in that direction, hiking higher and higher into the mountains. As they reach their target, they find the ruins of a small city. Traversing the carcass of this once-great center of learning is not without its dangers, but the two persevere and find themselves at the imposing doors of the Library. Inside, they learn of the origins of the Artifact and the philosophy of Oneness which came to dominate life in the Academy. In the highest levels of the Library, where the records indicate that the ancient scientists had their central insight, Scarlet pulls a lever. Unseen gearworks groan and complain as the observatory ceiling struggles open. A tremor shakes the skeletal remains of the library as spectral starlight shines down upon the wanderers. As Slate urges them to leave, Scarlet looks up peacefully and says: "There is only Being. All being is one, and all beings are one." She disappears. Slate only has a moment to react before the Library starts collapsing. He shakes off his surprise and dashes back the way he came, falling to his knees outside the structure only moments its total failure. He looks up to see Scarlet, perfectly calm. "I'll see you back home," she says, and disappears.

Much later, Slate arrives back at the Academy. He's ushered in with a hero's welcome. The Artifact is back! Bewildered, he enters the chamber of the Artifact and finds it floating in its customary place. His entourage return to their day's meditation, and Slate is left alone with the Artifact. He approaches it slowly.

"Scarlet?"

## Key Characters

### Scarlet

Scarlet lives in quiet reflection in a society where distinctions like "young" and "woman" are rarely mentioned. She looks like most Academics: dark-skinned, bald, and with barely a visual indication of gender. At the start of our story, she seems perfectly content with her life. After the incident with the Artifact, however, she begins to experience powerful emotions as she learns more about the world and herself. She's distraught by the thought that her actions are causing so much harm to those back at the Academy, and this drive to help is what pushes her forward.

### Slate

Slate is one of a handful of Academics tasked with bringing Scarlet back and solving the mystery of the Artifact's disappearance. He has no desire to be outside the Academy and sees danger in everything foreign. He's rigid and unyielding. Working with Scarlet is almost unbearable at first, though the two develop a respect and trust for one another through their journey and their shared goal of helping the Academy.

### Non-Player Characters

#### Kuluun

Kuluun is a Traveler farmer. He's made a life for himself in the merchant town near the Academy by selling his cacti and trading with other Travelers who pass through. As the owner of one of the few permanent Traveler establishments, he takes Scarlet in after she's collapses near the outpost. Though gruff and finding Scarlet almost completely alien, he shows her kindness. He's good-natured at heart.

## Travel, Time, and Weather

* The player is presented with snapshots of the world in each level, which they progress through linearly. There is no open exploration.
* Each level is static: no time passes, weather doesn't change. However, each level presents a very different view of the world: desert outpost, forest wilderness, sunken temple, ancient ruin.

# Mechanics

As Scarlet gains each ability, she also gains the ability to see that color. The game starts in black and white, and the color of the world "fills in" as the player makes her way through the game. This reflects the narrative in that the world becomes more colorful literally as Scarlet learns that life is much more "colorful" than the one of meditation she knew at the Academy. The journey teaches Scarlet fear, joy, anger, excitement, and much more.

Scarlet's interaction with the Artifact allows her to manipulate frequencies of light. She does so by "resonating" at a specific frequency, which the player sees as a shift in the color of the world. The main movement mechanics are:

* Dash, red, fire
* Stomp, yellow, earth
* Double-jump, green, air
* The final ability has not yet been chosen, but it's associated with blue and water. Some options we're considering include swinging or teleporting. Further prototyping is needed.

After using an ability, Scarlet sees the world in the frequency of that ability for a short time. When the world changes color, elements of the world are revealed or hidden. For example, a red platform is invisible if the world is red. That means a red wall can be walked through (yay!) but a red platform can't be landed on (no!).

Scarlet uses these abilities to traverse environmental puzzles. These puzzles sometimes require quick reflexes to rapidly shift between colors. For example, she might need to shift to green to double jump over a high wall, then shift to red to dash mid-air through a red wall/opening, and finally shift to yellow before landing to stomp through a weak wooden floor. The levels are mostly linear, with little to no exploration. The focus is on environmental puzzle-solving.

# Development

Scarlet will be our team's first game. We simply want to produce and release something quickly, so we can gain the experience of the full production cycle. We will not be attempting anything overly complex. We are therefore purposefully constraining the scope, budget, and timeline as follows:

* Design a game that can be built and released in three months.
* All team members are working on the game on the side.
* We have a maximum $500 budget for art assets or Unity plugins.

## Key Personnel

* Designer (D): Responsible for game design, story, and development
* Programmer (P): Responsible for development, programming, and design
* Artist (A): Responsible for art, design, and development

As you can see, all personnel must be cross-functional to some degree. We have already filled the roles of Designer and Programmer (I will be the Designer). We are still searching for an Artist. This is our first game, so no specific experience is required beyond the basics of the roles (i.e. Artist can make pretty pictures).

## Budget

As mentioned above, we have up to $500 to spend on art assets or Unity plugins. Priority will be given to items that would take the most time to implement ourselves. Time is precious.

## Production Timeline

Due to our constraints, the game will have no more than four core mechanics. There will be six levels: one introductory level, one level for each mechanic, and one final level. The estimates in the timeline below are based on full-time work. Since we are all working part-time, a 3x multiplier was used as a buffer. It gives a total estimate of 32 days broken out as follows:

* 11 days for prototyping
* 7 days for playtesting
* 14 days for polish (including additional playtesting)

## Appendix B: Discarded Level 1 Concept Script

*This script is from a previous version of the game called Violet, set in space. Violet is a space pirate.*

Red lights flash, glowing hotly in the metal surface of the walls, the floor. VIOLET walks down the halls of a space ship. This is a pirate vessel, all maverick patchwork and grit. A calm voice over an intercom:

THE CAPTAIN
We're almost in position. All breachers to your designated chute.

The player now takes control of Violet, learning how to move her back and forward. The ship jerks violently, and a couple crates tumble across the hallway floor.

THE PILOT
Umm, evasive maneuvers…

The player now learns how to jump. Violet humps over the crates and continues down the hallway. A door opens at her approach and she enters the breaching chute. A circular hatch is in the middle of the ceiling. Various pirates sound off on the intercom, announcing that they are in position. The hatch on the ceiling shines an X on floor. Violet moves to the center of the X.

VIOLET
Violet, in position.

The camera tracks up to reveal that on the other side of the hatch on the ceiling is outer space. Across an expanse of debris is another ship. In her chute, Violet touches a hand to her chest. A holographic helmet appears around her head, and small rocket boosters erupt from her boots. She floats off the ground slightly now.

THE CAPTAIN
Launching in 3, 2, 1…

The hatch opens, and Violet is sucked into the vacuum. The player dodges the space debris as Violet hurtles towards the other ship. As she nears it, Violet spins 180 and the camera turns with her. Her rocket boots fire and she slows, thudding onto the side of the ship. Violet's boots emit a flash of light.

VIOLET
Mag tether engaged.

Violet runs across the surface of the ship. Defense guns fire lasers at her, which the player must jump to avoid. The player learns how to dash and double-jump using the rocket boots. Violet makes her way to a hatch in the side of the ship. It opens and closes regularly. The player times their jump and enters the ship.

VIOLET
I'm in.

THE CAPTAIN
Good job, Violet. Now grab some loot and get out of there.

Violet explores the ship, which has branching hallways and many rooms. Some of the items the player can find include food in a store room, weapons in an armory, and shiny platinum currency. Violet has a backpack which fills up as she collects items.

THE CAPTAIN
Time's up. Let's clear out before backup gets here.

As the player heads back to where Violet entered the ship, there's an open door we haven't seen before. Something is glowing inside. Violet, intrigued, enters.

THE ARTIFACT floats in the center of the room. It's a small globe, about the size of a bowling ball, and it emits a clean, white light. Violet approaches it slowly. Another pirate, SCRATCH, enters the room.

SCRATCH
Didn't you hear the captain? It's time to go. Woah, that looks… expensive.

VIOLET
What is it?

SCRATCH
How should I know? Just grab it and let's go! Reinforcements are here.

As Violet reaches for it, a blinding flash of light. Violet is knocked away and collapses to the ground. Scratch looks around as alarms go off.

VIOLET
Help…

SCRATCH
Shit!

Scratch runs. Soldiers in gleaming gray enter the room, weapons drawn. Violet blacks out.
