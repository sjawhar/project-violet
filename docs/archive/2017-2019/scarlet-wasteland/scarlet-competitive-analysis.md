# Executive Summary

Oneness is the truth of the Academy, the only home that Scarlet has ever known. A life of simple, quiet meditation. Everything is… gray. Until an accidental brush with a powerful Artifact transports Scarlet to a world filled with color. Searching for a way back home, Scarlet jumps, dashes, slams, swings, and slides across strange and vibrant lands. Along the way, she's forced to confront the truth behind stories she's so used to telling about the world and herself.

Scarlet is a 2D platforming search for truth and the self. As the first game of an amateur studio, it will be released under the PWYW model to gain the trust of the gaming public. It will find its audience by clearly positioning itself as a story-driven game with a unique message of oneness and by leveraging the the success and appeal of other story-driven indie platformers such as GRIS and Celeste.

# Competition

To understand the competition, we have to consider the publishing channels available to us. Those are the shop windows in which we'll be competing for space. Some platforms, like Kongregate, are focused on F2P games and subsidized by ad revenue. I find this model unsustainable for the industry as it devalues the product. Instead, I prefer the pay what you want (PWYW) model. As a first time game developer, I honestly don't know what my game is worth or if it's worth anything at all, and neither does the player until they've actually played it. If the community supports the game and it becomes successful, then other publishing platforms can be pursued at significantly lower risk (they have a much higher barrier to entry).

Now that we've chosen PWYW stores, some naturally rise to the top: Humble Bundle and Itch.io especially, but Game Jolt as well. Finally, even though it's not PWYW, GOG is definitely worth considering for two reasons: no submission fees, and marketing support.

Now that we've chosen our stores, we can do a survey of our competition. As we're specifically targeting PWYW stores, many of the top games have no fixed price. Those that do tend to fall in the $4-$9 range.

Though it would perhaps be overly optimistic of the quality of our first game to compare it to the leading indie platformers on these stores, those are the titles with which we will be competing for players' attention. Let us therefore examine a couple in more detail.

## Competition: GRIS

GRIS was announced and released in 2018. It has a powerful message to convey about a young woman overcoming grief, and the developers wanted to make sure that anyone can play it. They therefore adopted an extremely unique "no death, no stress" approach to gameplay. The game is quite short - in the three hour range - and the platforming itself has been described as fairly typical. Nonetheless, critics have been raving about emotionally powerful story which the developers have conveyed with exceptional visual and sound direction and not a single line of dialogue. Users' reviews are just as positive as the critics'. GRIS sells for $16.99.

From GRIS, we learn that a great platformer does not necessarily need complex gameplay. Strong art direction and a purposeful story can make for a powerful, if short, experience.

## Competition: Celeste

Unlike GRIS, Celeste is being praised for its challenging platforming, exceptional puzzle design, and tight controls. Like GRIS, though, Celeste has a powerful story to tell. On the surface it is about a young woman climbing a mountain, but more deeply it is a profound discussion of mental illness. That is by no means an easy topic, and the developers deserve respect for their nuanced handling of the issue. Celeste sells for $19.99.

# Differentiation

GRIS and Celeste are recognized as two of the best games of the year. Both are indie platformers with a powerful message. Both have female main characters facing an internal struggle. Both are in the $15-20 range. It's clear that there is both a commercial market and a critical audience for such a game. The issue then becomes one of positioning Scarlet to stand out.

The core concept must be unique. There is a growing interest in mindfulness in the zeitgeist. Though there seems to be a new meditation app popping up every other day, I'm not aware of any games that explore the theme. Scarlet's core message of mindfulness and oneness might therefore help it attract attention.

Once we have a potential player's attention, we need to convince them to give Scarlet a try. Being PWYW will help by significantly lowering the barrier to entry for an interested player.

# Audience

This audience Scarlet will be competing for is not one of dedicated platform players. Instead, our target player looks for a game that is minimalist and story-driven. It guides the player through a particular type of personal experience. It does not focus on twitch gameplay, deep combat, punishing difficulty, or open worlds. The player's favorite game might be To the Moon, Never Alone, Life is Strange, Journey, or Ori and the Blind Forest. Their most highly anticipated upcoming game might be In the Valley of Gods. They find such value in the atmosphere and message of a game that they might not even notice whether the gameplay is mundane or exceptional. They are exhausted by endless franchise entries and crave something more meaningful. We will release Scarlet to serve that player.
