# High Concept Statement

Oneness is the truth of the Academy, the only home that Scarlet has ever known. A life of simple, quiet meditation. Everything is… gray. Until an accidental brush with a powerful Artifact transports Scarlet to a world filled with color. Searching for a way back home, Scarlet jumps, dashes, slams, swings, and slides across strange and vibrant lands. Along the way, she's forced to grapple with the stories she's so used to telling about the world and herself.

Scarlet is a 2D platforming search for truth and the self. As my first game, it will be released under the PWYW model to gain the trust of the gaming public. It will find its audience by clearly positioning itself as a story-driven game with a unique exploration of oneness and the self.

# Feature Set

As Scarlet gains each platforming ability, she also gains the ability to see that color. The game starts in black and white, and the color of the world "fills in" as the player makes her way through the game. This reflects the narrative in that the world becomes more colorful literally as Scarlet learns that life is much more "colorful" than the one of meditation she knew at the Academy. The journey teaches Scarlet fear, joy, anger, excitement, and much more.

Scarlet's interaction with the Artifact allows her to manipulate frequencies of light. She does so by "resonating" at a specific frequency, which the player sees as a shift in the color of the world. The main movement mechanics are:

* Dash, red, fire
* Stomp, yellow, earth
* Double-jump, green, air
* The final ability has not yet been chosen, but it's associated with blue and water. Some options I'm considering include swinging or teleporting. Further prototyping is needed.

After using an ability, Scarlet (and the player) sees the world in the frequency of that ability for a short time. When the world changes color, elements of the world are revealed or hidden. For example, a red platform is invisible if the world is red. That means a red wall can be walked through (yay!) but a red platform can't be landed on (no!).

Scarlet uses these abilities to traverse environmental puzzles. These puzzles sometimes require quick reflexes to rapidly shift between colors. For example, she might need to shift to green to double jump over a high wall, then shift to red to dash mid-air through a red wall/opening, and finally shift to yellow before landing to stomp through a weak wooden floor. The levels are mostly linear, with little to no exploration. The focus is on environmental puzzle-solving.

# Design Influences

GRIS and Celeste are recognized as two of the best games of 2018. Both are indie platformers with a powerful message. Both have female main characters facing an internal struggle. Both are in the $15-20 range. It's clear that there is both a commercial market and a critical audience for such a game. The issue then becomes one of positioning Scarlet to stand out.

The core concept must be unique. There is a growing interest in mindfulness in the zeitgeist. Though there seems to be a new meditation app popping up every other day, I'm not aware of any games that explore the theme. Scarlet's core message of mindfulness and oneness might therefore help it attract attention.

Once we have a potential player's attention, we need to convince them to give Scarlet a try. Being PWYW will help by significantly lowering the barrier to entry for an interested player.

In many ways, this game is competing not so much with other platformers as with other minimalist, story-driven indie games. The goal of Scarlet is to guide the player through a particular type of personal experience. We are not focusing on twitch gameplay, deep combat, punishing difficulty, or open worlds. As such, we also draw inspiration from games like To the Moon, Never Alone, Life is Strange, Journey, Firewatch, and Ori and the Blind Forest.

# Scope Management

I'm limited by my experience, so I'm going to try to keep the gameplay and technical aspects of the game as simple as possible. I will not be developing mechanics more complex than acrobatics-based platforming. Combat is probably off the table, and possibly enemies or damage as well. Open world design is probably beyond my abilities at the moment, so the levels will be very linear. Inventory is unlikely. I'm really going to focus on solid story and fun, simple, but challenging environmental puzzles.
