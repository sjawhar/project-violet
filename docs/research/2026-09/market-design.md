# Violet — Design Landscape & Market Research (as of Sept 2026)

## 1. Ranked Recommendation

**Position Violet as a narrative Metroidvania-lite where each of 4 colors is simultaneously a movement ability *and* a visibility/passability toggle, with compounding (not exclusive) traversal — closer to Hollow Knight/Ori's ability-gated exploration than to Hue's pure-puzzle or Ikaruga/Outland's pure-combat polarity systems.** No existing title occupies this combination. Hue (2016) proved "recolor the world, matching elements vanish" sells well (92% positive, 1,680+ reviews) but never attached it to a character ability or narrative weight — its story was panned as an afterthought. Ikaruga (2001) and its descendant Outland (2011) proved color/polarity gating works for platforming *and* combat, but both are mechanically legible rather than emotionally resonant ("dull story" cited against Outland). GRIS/Neva/Cocoon/Chants of Sennaar proved 2018–2026 audiences pay $15–25 for tightly-scoped (4–10hr), near-wordless emotional platformers with sales from 100K to 3M+ units — but none use color-as-ability at all. Violet fuses three separately-proven value propositions nobody else has combined. Scope benchmark (Section 8): a 6–10hr campaign built by a small human core (3–8 people) leveraging an AI-agent swarm for iteration volume, $19.99–$24.99, 2.5–4yr timeline — GRIS's team/timeline with Silksong's "small core, huge polish" ethic.

| Title (Year, Studio) | Mechanic expressing theme | Reception |

**Takeaway:** no notable title pairs explicit oneness/mindfulness theming with genuine platforming *challenge* — all four above are low/no-failure-state exploration pieces. Violet combining hard Celeste/Ori-tier movement with this theme is unclaimed territory.

### Movement-ability design lessons (Celeste / Ori / Hollow Knight)
- **Celeste:** built from a tiny 3-verb set (jump, climb/grab, 8-way dash refilled by "green gems"), born from a 2016 PICO-8 jam whose hard limits, per co-creator Noel Berry, "forced us to find the core." GDC 2017's "Level Design Workshop: Designing Celeste" (Thorson) shows >300 rooms built from permutation, not addition. The team *permitted* rather than patched out emergent tech ("demodash," "corner boosting," a "hyper jump" homage from Thorson's earlier TowerFall) — tolerating skilled exploitation grows speedrun/challenge engagement without hand-authoring it.
- **Ori:** "Bash" (freeze time, launch self + target in opposite directions) is the most complex ability in either game, taught via a dedicated low-stakes tutorial, then layered onto jump/floaty-jump/double-jump/air-dash as a fifth "jump" type. Lesson: teach each ability alone, then immediately re-combine it with everything learned before — the *combinatorial* space, not the raw ability count, is what deepens play. GDC 2021 "Crafting the World of Ori and the Will of the Wisps" (gdcvault.com/play/1027071) and a companion "Art of Ori" talk (gdcvault.com/play/1027375) cover this co-design process.
- **Hollow Knight:** unlike Celeste/Ori's linear drops, Team Cherry permits non-linear acquisition/sequence-breaking (e.g. multiple routes to the Dream Nail), "gently limiting" rather than hard-gating one path — each area stays challenge-appropriate "even if reached early or late." This is the closest precedent for Violet's 4-color unlock structure if color order is player-driven: design every area to stay fair regardless of which color arrives first.

## 4. Linux + AMD-iGPU Compatibility Notes

**[INFERENCE]** — none of these titles were tested on this devbox. Hue, ChromaGun, Splice, Celeste, GRIS, Neva, Cocoon, Tunic, and Animal Well are lightweight 2D/2.5D (Unity, custom, or GameMaker-class) with no discrete-GPU dependency; all should run acceptably on the Radeon 890M iGPU via Proton/native Linux, since none use ray tracing, heavy compute shaders, or CUDA. Ikaruga, Outland, and Kromaia are older, modest-requirement 3D/2.5D titles, similarly low-risk. If Violet adopts a Unity-class or 2D-native engine (per the parallel EngineResearch workstream) rather than an Unreal/CUDA-heavy pipeline, dev-machine parity across the AI-agent swarm and human testers on this hardware should not be a blocker — untested, and should be validated once an engine is chosen.

## 5. Agent-Operability of the Genre's Toolchain

These titles show mixed agent-friendliness. GRIS/Neva (Nomada) and Cocoon (Geometric) used conventional engines (Unity-class) with hand-authored, non-diffable binary scenes — a poor fit for a text/diff-first agent swarm without extra tooling (e.g. a custom level-data DSL). Tunic and Animal Well were built on fully custom engines by their solo creators specifically to iterate faster outside Unity/Unreal — Basso built his own engine and modified Aseprite — showing text-legible tooling (level editors emitting JSON/YAML, not binary scenes) is achievable at small-team scale and would meaningfully improve agent operability for Violet's levels, art, and playtesting pipelines. No title surveyed used MCP/agent-native tooling in production (all predate or are merely contemporaneous with agentic AI) — Violet would be a first-mover applying an agent swarm to this genre, not following an established pattern.

## 6. Licensing, Commercial Rights & Steam AI-Disclosure Implications

Steam's Generative AI disclosure policy (Jan 2024, clarified Jan 17 2026) requires a Steamworks Content Survey section distinguishing **pre-generated AI content** shipped in the game (art/code/sound/narrative — disclosed publicly on the store page) from **live-generated AI content** (e.g. dynamic NPC dialogue, requiring documented "guardrails," with AI-generated Adult-Only sexual content absolutely banned). The 2026 update exempts AI **development efficiency tools** (coding assistants, brainstorming, Photoshop generative fill) from disclosure as long as output isn't shipped/consumed by players. **Implication for Violet's agent-swarm plan:** using Claude/GPT agents for code, level iteration, QA, and tooling is disclosure-exempt; generative AI for *shipped* art, music, VO, or narrative text is not, and needs public disclosure plus a stated-tooling summary — a real reputational risk given current sentiment (Section 8). Valve has rejected games on AI-training-provenance grounds even when disclosed, so any AI-assisted asset pipeline should track model provenance defensively. No licensing dispute over "color as mechanic" surfaced anywhere in this research (Hue vs. Ikaruga vs. ChromaGun coexist) — it is an unprotectable game-design idea, not an IP risk for Violet.

## 7. Costs

No paid-license costs are implicated (no engine/asset-store fee discovered as a blocker). Analytics tooling used throughout this research (Gamalytic, SteamDB, Video Game Insights/Sensor Tower) is free-tier/public for basic queries; deeper per-title analytics (e.g. Gamalytic Pro) would carry subscription cost only if the team wants ongoing competitive tracking. Steam Direct listing fee is $100/game — a rounding error against any real budget, but notable as a floor: the median Q1 2026 release earned ~$250 lifetime, meaning roughly half of new Steam releases don't clear their own listing fee.

## 8. Risks / Unknowns

- **Evidentiary gaps [INFERENCE flagged inline]:** several prices, dev-time windows, and scores (Celeste, Ori, Spiritfarer, Tunic, Sennaar, Cocoon, Planet of Lana) weren't confirmed from one authoritative source; third-party sales tools (Gamalytic, Raijin, Sensor Tower, Steam Revenue Calculator) disagree 2–4x on the same title (Tunic: 320K vs. 746K units) — treat any Violet sales projection as directional, not precise.
- **AI-sentiment survey disagreement is itself a risk:** Quantic Foundry (Dec 2025, ~2M respondents) found 85%+ negative on generative AI, worsening over time (46%→77–83% negative on AI dialogue, 2024→2025), while GameDiscoverCo (July 2026, 3,800 players) found 43% "fine with it"/neutral — likely a framing/sample-selection difference. Plan messaging for the more skeptical scenario: ~90% of surveyed buyers reportedly check AI disclaimers before purchase, and even *false* AI accusations have caused review-bombing and cancellations (Running With Scissors, late 2025).
- **Genre crowding is accelerating:** Next Fest demo count rose 66% YoY (2,645→4,382, June'25→June'26); Steam releases are pacing ~27,000+ for 2026 vs. 20,658 in 2025 — discovery is measurably harder than a year ago, reinforcing the case for Violet's differentiated hook and early, sustained wishlist-building over a cold launch.
- **Silksong's Chinese-localization stumble** shows even a beloved franchise takes a real review-score hit from bad non-English localization — QA every shipped language, not just English.
- **Franchise-fatigue signal:** Planet of Lana II underperformed the original on launch day — emotional-platformer audiences don't auto-carry hype like action franchises; don't assume linear sequel growth.
- **Unverified for this devbox:** no title here was actually run on Radeon 890M hardware; Section 4's compatibility notes are inferred from genre/engine class, not measured — validate once an engine is chosen (handoff to EngineResearch).
