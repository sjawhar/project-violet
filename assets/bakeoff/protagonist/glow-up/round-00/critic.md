# Visual Quality

**Score: 2/5**

The rendering reads as an early pass, not a finished asset. Shading on the robe and legs is a scribbly cross-hatch texture rather than the smooth painterly gradients or clean flat cel-shading of GRIS/Planet of Lana. The palette is a single muted tan/brown/gray/white — there's no atmospheric color depth, rim lighting, or material variation (cloth vs. skin vs. hair) that either bar title uses to make a character feel rendered rather than sketched. The scarf's trailing edges are jagged/frayed like a torn flag rather than a deliberately shaped flowing ribbon.

# Character Appeal

**Score: 2/5**

Poses are stiff and several animations are barely differentiated: jump, fall, and double_jump (rows 3–5 on the contact sheet) show almost the same arms-up, one-leg-bent silhouette, so the character doesn't feel like it's doing something distinct in the air each time. The face is an unreadable smudge with no visible eyes or expression in any of the 42 frames, so there's no emotional signal at all — a big miss next to Lana's expressive close-ups or even GRIS's minimal but legible face shape. Arm/scarf secondary motion is present but doesn't add much life; the run cycle in particular looks a little floaty/mechanical rather than weighted.

# Color Readability

**Score: 3/5**

This is a character-only sheet, so judged on scarf + silhouette alone: the big shapes (hood, robe, trailing scarf) are clean and the white scarf pops clearly against the neutral gray. But the lower legs/ankles/hem are rendered as a noisy scribble rather than a solid shape in the run, jump, fall, double_jump, and dash rows, which fractures the silhouette right where a player's eye tracks footing and landings — the one place readability matters most for a platformer.

# Three Biggest Gaps vs. the Bar

1. **Jump, fall, and double_jump share nearly one pose.** Rows 3, 4, and 5 of the contact sheet are visually near-identical (arms raised, one knee bent) — a player can't tell rising from falling from double-jumping at a glance, whereas GRIS/Lana give each air-state a distinct, deliberate silhouette.
2. **No face.** Every frame across all seven animations renders the face as a flat brown smudge with no eyes or brow — zero expressiveness, which kills "alive" character appeal that both bar titles lean on heavily (Lana's readable expressions, GRIS's minimal but clear features).
3. **Scribble-texture legs break the silhouette.** In run, jump, fall, double_jump, and dash, the lower legs/ankles are rendered as loose cross-hatch noise instead of a clean flat or painted shape, so the silhouette goes fuzzy exactly at the feet — the region that needs to read cleanest for platforming footing/landing cues.
